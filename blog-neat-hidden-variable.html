Metabolism

NEAT: the hidden variable that quietly shapes your metabolism

Most people focus on workouts when they think about energy expenditure. But the movement you do outside the gym often matters just as much.

What NEAT is

NEAT stands for non-exercise activity thermogenesis. It includes the calories you burn through ordinary movement: walking, standing, carrying groceries, pacing, and fidgeting.

It may seem minor, but the total effect is massive over the course of a day.

Why it matters

When people compare their energy expenditure, they often overlook how much daily movement differs from person to person. Two people can follow similar workout plans and still burn very different amounts of energy simply because one is far more active outside training.

  • More daily movement improves glucose handling.
  • It supports cardiovascular health and metabolic resilience.
  • It helps create a sustainable calorie deficit without depending solely on extra workouts.

Simple ways to improve it

Small changes work best. The goal is to make movement more automatic, not to create a second full-time job.

  • Stand during calls or meetings.
  • Take a short walk after meals.
  • Park farther away and choose stairs when possible.
  • Break up long sitting blocks every hour.

Putting it into practice

If you want to improve body composition or health, build a life that supports movement even on low-motivation days. That is often more sustainable than trying to “make up” for a sedentary day with a hard workout.
