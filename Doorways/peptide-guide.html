Peptides 101

What exactly are peptides?

Peptides are chains of amino acids, the same building blocks that make up proteins. Our bodies naturally produce many peptides that help regulate important biological processes. Some act as hormones or signaling molecules, helping cells communicate with one another.

Researchers have also developed peptide-based medications that target specific receptors and pathways. Depending on the compound, these pathways may be involved in:

Appetite and glucose regulationHormonal signalingTissue repair and cellular processesMetabolismOther physiological functions

But here's something important: not all peptides work the same way, and not all have the same level of scientific evidence.

Some peptide-based medications have undergone extensive human clinical trials and received FDA approval for specific conditions. Others remain investigational. And some compounds widely discussed online have very little reliable human safety or effectiveness data.

Three terms worth understanding

FDA-approved

A specific medication has undergone regulatory review for its approved use.

Investigational

A compound is being studied, but its safety or effectiveness for a proposed use has not been established sufficiently for approval.

Compounded

A medication is specially prepared under applicable pharmacy requirements. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved.

Understanding these differences is one of the most important parts of making an informed decision.

Metabolic Health & Body Composition

Why GLP-1 medications are getting so much attention

GLP-1-based medications have changed the conversation around obesity treatment and metabolic health, and for good reason. Clinical trials have demonstrated substantial average weight reductions with certain medications when used in studied populations. These treatments interact with biological pathways involved in appetite, satiety, and glucose regulation.

Semaglutide

ACTIVATES GLP-1 RECEPTORS
14.9%

Average weight reduction with semaglutide 2.4 mg over 68 weeks in the STEP 1 trial, vs. 2.4% in the placebo group.

Tirzepatide

ACTIVATES GIP + GLP-1 RECEPTORS
15–20.9%

Average weight reductions across studied doses over 72 weeks in the SURMOUNT-1 trial, vs. 3.1% with placebo.

Retatrutide

TARGETS GIP, GLP-1 + GLUCAGON
Investigational

A triple-receptor agonist. Clinical research has demonstrated promising weight-loss results, but retatrutide remains investigational as of this guide's October 2026 review.

These findings are meaningful. But medications aren't appropriate for everyone, and potential adverse effects, contraindications, and long-term treatment planning matter. See how each medication works, receptor by receptor.

Significant weight loss can include reductions in both fat mass and lean tissue. That's one reason resistance training and adequate nutrition deserve attention during weight management.

Beyond Weight Loss

What about recovery, hormones, and longevity?

This is where peptide conversations get especially interesting, and where we need to be particularly careful about separating potential from proof.

Recovery and tissue repair

Compounds such as BPC-157 and TB-500 are frequently discussed for tendon, ligament, and soft-tissue recovery.

But much of the research is preclinical, and reliable human evidence is limited. These compounds have not been established as safe and effective treatments for routine musculoskeletal injuries.

Growth hormone pathways

Sermorelin, CJC-1295, and ipamorelin influence pathways related to growth hormone signaling.

However, increasing growth hormone levels does not automatically mean improving strength, recovery, or healthy aging. The evidence does not support treating these compounds as proven general anti-aging solutions.

Women's hormonal health

Peptides such as kisspeptin are being investigated for reproductive hormone signaling.

Bremelanotide has an FDA-approved indication for acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women. But peptides are not interchangeable with evidence-based menopause treatments.

Energy and longevity

MOTS-c and humanin have attracted interest because of their roles in mitochondrial biology. Most proposed wellness and longevity benefits remain unproven in humans.

Elamipretide has a specific FDA-approved rare-disease indication, not a general anti-aging indication.

THE BIGGER PICTURE
Emerging research can be exciting. But promising doesn't mean proven, and natural doesn't automatically mean safe.

You can be curious about what's next without treating every new compound as an established therapy.

Safety, Quality & Access

Before you consider a peptide, know what you're getting.

This may be the most important part of the guide, because the compound itself is only part of the conversation. Where a product comes from, how it's manufactured, and whether its use is medically appropriate all matter.

FDA-approved vs. compounded vs. research-only

FDA-approved

Has undergone regulatory review for specific indications.

Compounded

Can serve legitimate medical needs, but not FDA-approved and subject to specific legal and quality requirements.

Research-use-only

Not automatically suitable for human use, even when sold with claims about purity.

Not FDA-approved doesn't mean unsafe, and FDA-approved doesn't mean risk-free. What matters is the evidence, the source, and qualified oversight.

What about certificates of analysis?

A certificate of analysis, or COA, may provide information about chemical identity, purity, or other tests. But a high purity percentage does not establish that an injectable product is sterile, free from harmful endotoxins, appropriately manufactured, or safe for human administration.

Purity isn't the same as sterility. And neither one proves clinical effectiveness.

Questions worth asking
  • Is this medication FDA-approved for the proposed use?
  • What human research supports it?
  • What are the known risks and unknowns?
  • Where will the medication be dispensed?
  • What medical evaluation and monitoring are appropriate?
  • What evidence-based alternatives are available?

Be cautious of anyone promising dramatic results without meaningful risks or limitations. And remember: access to a product isn't proof that it's appropriate for you.

Protecting Muscle: Our Favorite Part

Because the number on the scale isn't the whole story.

Let's talk about something we believe deserves more attention in conversations about metabolic health: muscle. Muscle matters for much more than appearance. It supports strength, mobility, glucose regulation, and physical independence. And when someone is losing a significant amount of weight, preserving muscle and physical function should be part of the conversation.

  1. Prioritize resistance training.

    For general health, aim to include muscle-strengthening activities at least twice per week. A well-designed program can be adjusted to your experience, abilities, and schedule. Consistency and appropriate progression matter more than chasing exhausting workouts.

  2. Eat adequate protein.

    Protein provides amino acids needed for muscle maintenance and repair. Many active adults may benefit from approximately 1.2 to 1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight daily, although individual needs vary.

    82–109 g per day for a 150-pound adult. A general educational example, not a prescription.

  3. Keep moving.

    Walking, running, cycling, and other forms of aerobic activity support cardiovascular health. You don't have to choose between lifting weights and doing cardio.

Your Questions, Answered

Five things we want you to know

Are peptides safe?

Safety depends on the specific compound, formulation, medical indication, and individual. Some peptide-based medications have substantial human clinical evidence. Others have major safety-data gaps.

Do peptides replace healthy habits?

No. Medications may provide benefits that lifestyle changes alone don't fully replicate, but they don't replace the value of strength training, nutrition, movement, or recovery.

Can peptides help with perimenopause?

Some peptides influence hormonal signaling, but investigational peptides have not been established as general treatments for menopausal symptoms. Evidence-based menopause care remains important.

Will I regain weight if I stop a GLP-1 medication?

Weight regain is common after discontinuing certain GLP-1-based weight-management treatments, although individual experiences vary. Discuss a long-term treatment plan with your clinician.

How do I know what information to trust?

Look for human clinical research, current regulatory information, transparent discussion of risks, and qualified medical guidance. Be cautious when marketing promises more than the evidence supports.
