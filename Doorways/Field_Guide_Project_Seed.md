## 1. Brand Architecture

**Umbrella brand:** The Health Field Guide
**Flagship offering (general population, working name):** The Health Guide *(name may change later — not locked)*
**Current active doorway (niche landing page, in progress):** The Field Guide — off-grid / overland / van life / homesteading avatar

Structure going forward: one umbrella brand, one flagship offer, multiple "doorway" landing pages that each speak to a specific avatar's pain points but funnel into the same membership tiers and the same provider (Clint). The off-grid page is the first doorway built. The flagship (general population) page is the next build, developed in tandem so the two share a consistent chassis rather than drifting apart stylistically.

**Core promise (applies to every doorway):**
> No matter where you are, or who the system has failed to explain things to, you have an expert in your corner.

**Positioning is "Guide," not "Concierge."** Concierge signals luxury and passivity — someone else solves it for you. Guide signals collaboration — you're still the one making decisions; the Guide makes sure you're not making them blind. This applies across every avatar, not just off-grid.

---

## 2. Core Voice & Messaging Pillars

- Protect your freedom / invest in yourself
- The second opinion you deserve
- Healing and fixing the problem — not just masking the symptom
- Understanding how the body works is the Guide's specialty, not the client's
- Google, AI, and Facebook groups don't know your body, your history, or what's actually going on — a real provider who knows you does
- Expert guidance, wherever the road takes you / whatever the system has failed to tell you

**Scope-of-practice guardrail (applies everywhere, not just off-grid):** Clint is a PT operating from a biopsychosocial model. He can and should **screen for and triage** psychological/stress factors that interact with a physical presentation, and coordinate referral to a licensed mental health provider when needed. He should not be positioned as providing clinical psychological *treatment* — that's a licensure line, not a marketing choice. FAQ and program copy should always say "screens for and loops in the right professional," never "provides psychological care."

## 3. Membership Tiers (Locked — same across all doorways for now)

**Tier 1 — Concierge Access — $49/month**
- Virtual medical screening & assessment whenever something comes up
- Guidance on what's next — self-care, urgent care, or ER
- Direct interface with treating providers (ER docs, specialists)
- A real follow-up plan after any incident

**Tier 2 — Ongoing Care & Performance — $199/month** *(includes everything in Tier 1, plus:)*

- Sleep, nutrition & lifestyle coaching
- Custom strength & conditioning programming

## 4. Reusable Page Chassis (the "bones" every doorway is built on)

Every doorway landing page follows this section order, matching the existing Emily amputee-coaching page structure on performancecollective.co (built in Duda):

1. **Hero** — eyebrow, headline, price line, subhead, proof paragraph, primary CTA, hero image
2. **Pain Points & Story** — headline naming the specific frustration, 3 bullets, bridge paragraph, testimonial quote, attribution, proof line
3. **Program & Pricing** — the two locked tiers, presented as "everything in Tier 1, plus" for Tier 2, soft-commitment CTA (free screening call, not a refund guarantee — acute triage doesn't map to Emily's 14-day pain-reduction guarantee)
4. **FAQ** — 5 Q&A pairs, doorway-specific objections plus the standing scope-of-practice answer
5. **Testimonials** — lead quote + reserved slot(s) for future member stories
6. **Footer** — global, unchanged across all doorways

**Duda implementation notes:** Duplicate the Emily page per doorway rather than building from scratch — preserves proven layout/spacing/mobile behavior. Only Hero, Pain Points, Program, FAQ, and Testimonials change; header, nav, and footer stay global.

## 5. Naming Research — Conflict Check Findings

Do not reuse these — real existing brand collisions found in the same or adjacent category:
- **RoamWell** — multiple existing travel-wellness/travel-medicine-kit brands, including a clinician-curated travel medicine kit sold on Amazon. Same audience, adjacent category.
- **Wayfinder** — an existing *Wayfinder Wellness and Physical Therapy* telehealth PT practice, plus Wayfinder Psychiatry and Wayfinder Health Strategies. Direct same-category collision.
- **Overland Health** — an existing UK telehealth/physio triage company operating on a biopsychosocial rehab model. Same name, same service category.
- **Basecamp** — globally known project-management software (37signals). Fine as an internal metaphor, not as a company name.
- **LoamWell** — not a collision, but a name that requires explanation costs marketing dollars every time it's used.

Clear / more available at time of check:
- **Field Guide** — no direct competing health/telehealth brand found. Most available and most distinctive option.
- **Overland Guide** — no direct collision, though "Overland" as a root is already owned by the overlanding media world (Overland Journal, Expedition Portal) and narrows toward 4x4/expedition identity specifically, which is narrower than the full off-grid/van-life/homesteading audience.

*(Reminder: this was a search-engine sweep, not a legal trademark clearance. Run an actual USPTO/domain check before final commitment.)*

## 6. Off-Grid Doorway — Finalized Content ("The Field Guide")

**Audience:** Full-time van/truck/RV/camper/homestead nomads, reliable cell/wifi reception, healthcare access and cost are the core pain points.

### Hero
- Eyebrow: YOUR FIELD GUIDE
- Headline: Protect Your Freedom. Keep Exploring — With Expert Guidance in Your Corner.
- Price line: $49/month Concierge Access · $199/month Ongoing Care & Performance
- Subhead: From "figure it out" to fully covered — without giving up the life you built off the grid.
- Proof paragraph: J. broke his arm a thousand miles from home. One video call caught the fracture, the nerve injury, and the tendon tear before he ever reached an ER — and he's still living the van life full time. See if the same guidance works for you.
- Primary CTA: See If Field Guide Is Right For You
- Hero image: truck exterior photo (Joshua Tree) or blurred interior photo

### Pain Points & Story
- Headline: You love the freedom of life off the grid. But when something happens, you're on your own.
- Bullets:
  - Falling off a ladder, a truck, or a roof — an hour from the nearest urgent care.
  - Not knowing if that headache, ankle, or back pain is "wait it out" or "go in now."
  - Telehealth built for a 9-to-5 life with an insurance card — not yours.
- Bridge: J. felt the same way. Until one video call changed how he thinks about staying safe out here.
- Testimonial: "He told me straight up I needed the ER — then gave me a list to hand the doctor. Every single thing on it turned out to be right."
- Attribution: — J., full-time box truck dweller, Field Guide member
- Proof line: J.'s outcome is exactly what this model is built to produce.

### FAQ
1. **Is this instead of a doctor or the ER?** No. Your Field Guide helps you figure out whether you need one — and stays in your corner if you do. In a true emergency, always go to the ER first.
2. **Do I need insurance for this?** No. Membership works the same whether you're parked in Utah or Maine, insured or not.
3. **What if I only need this occasionally?** That's exactly what Concierge Access is for.
4. **Can I move between tiers?** Yes — many members start on Concierge Access and move up once a specific injury or goal needs a structured plan.
5. **Does this cover nutrition, sleep, and mental health too?** Ongoing Care & Performance includes sleep, nutrition, and lifestyle coaching, plus strength and conditioning. For the mental load of this lifestyle, your Field Guide screens for it and loops in the right licensed professional when needed — the same biopsychosocial approach used in modern physical therapy.

### Testimonials

- Testimonial 1: "He told me straight up I needed the ER — then gave me a list to hand the doctor. Every single thing on it turned out to be right. Now I know exactly who to call before I panic." — J., full-time box truck dweller

## 8. Flagship Doorway — Early Draft ("The Health Guide")

**Status: first pass, not yet approved or built out to full page length.** Captured here so the new project can pick up exactly where this one left off.

**Audience (broad, multi-segment):** union workers, teachers/educators, rural and suburban populations, people the existing healthcare system has underserved or rushed through, the over-40 crowd wanting a clear plan regardless of geography. Unlike the off-grid avatar, the core objection isn't lack of access — it's access that exists but isn't working (rushed appointments, symptom-masking instead of root-cause fixes, no real explanation of what's happening in their body).

- Eyebrow: Your Field Guide
- Headline: Understanding how your body works is my specialty. Not yours.
- Subhead: Google has information. Your Facebook group has opinions. Neither one knows your body, your history, or what's actually going on. I do.
- Pain point headline: You're not imagining it. The system doesn't have time to actually explain anything.
- Bullets:
  - A 12-minute appointment isn't enough time to explain what's happening in your body, let alone why.
  - "Here's a prescription" isn't the same as "here's why this happened, and how we actually fix it."
  - You're left Googling symptoms at midnight, more confused than when you started.
- Testimonial (pulled from what people already say to Clint in real life): "I wish you lived closer — I would totally come to you for treatment."
- Positioning line: Healing and fixing the problem. Not just masking the symptom.
