The Reality

There's no shortage of information. It's hard to know what to trust.

01

Hype moves faster than research.

Social media treats a promising mechanism as if it were a proven result. Most of the time those are very different things.

02

The scale isn't the whole story.

Significant weight loss can include lean tissue. If nobody's watching your strength and function, you can lose more than you meant to.

03

Where it comes from matters.

FDA-approved, compounded, and “research-use-only” are not interchangeable, and a purity certificate doesn't prove a product is sterile or safe to inject.

Safety, Quality & Access

Before you consider a peptide, know what you're getting.

The compound is only part of the picture. Where it comes from, how it's made, and whether it's medically appropriate for you all matter.

FDA-approved

Has been through regulatory review for specific indications. That doesn't make it risk-free.

Compounded

Prepared by a pharmacy under specific legal and quality requirements. It can meet real medical needs, but it is not FDA-approved.

Research-use-only

Not automatically suitable for human use, whatever purity claims come with it. Purity isn't the same as sterility.

Questions worth asking
  • Is this FDA-approved for the proposed use?
  • What human research supports it?
  • What are the known risks and unknowns?
  • Where will it be dispensed?
  • What evaluation and monitoring are appropriate?
  • What evidence-based alternatives are available?
Protecting Muscle

Lose the weight. Keep the strength.

Muscle supports strength, mobility, glucose regulation, and independence. When weight comes off fast, holding on to it has to be part of the plan, and that part is what we do every day.

01

Prioritize resistance training.

At least two strength sessions a week, built around your experience, abilities, and schedule. Consistency and steady progression matter more than exhausting workouts.

02

Eat enough protein.

Many active adults do well at roughly 1.2–1.6 g per kg of body weight per day. For a 150 lb adult that's about 82–109 g. This is an example, not a prescription.

03

Keep moving.

Walking, running, and cycling all support cardiovascular health. You don't have to choose between lifting and cardio.

Questions

What people usually ask first

Are peptides safe?

It depends on the specific compound, formulation, medical indication, and person. Some peptide-based medications have substantial human clinical evidence. Others have major gaps in safety data.

Do peptides or GLP-1s replace healthy habits?

No. Medications can provide benefits that lifestyle changes alone may not, but they don't replace strength training, nutrition, movement, or recovery. Those work together.

Can peptides help with perimenopause?

Some peptides influence hormonal signaling, but investigational peptides haven't been established as general treatments for menopausal symptoms. Evidence-based menopause care still matters.

Will I regain weight if I stop a GLP-1 medication?

Regain is common after stopping certain GLP-1-based treatments, though experiences vary. A long-term plan, including strength and nutrition habits, should be part of the conversation with your clinician from the start.
